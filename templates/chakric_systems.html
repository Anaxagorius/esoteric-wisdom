Chakric Systems

The luminous wheels of vital force — the centres of consciousness through which subtle energy flows, transforms, and rises to transcendence.

What Are Chakras?

The word chakra (Sanskrit: चक्र — cakra) means “wheel” or “circle”, and in the esoteric anatomy of the Indian Tantric tradition, chakras are spinning vortices of subtle energy located at specific points along the subtle body’s central axis. They are not physical structures visible to ordinary senses but are understood to be perceivable by the trained clairvoyant as luminous, multi-petalled lotus flowers of various colours and vibration rates, each rotating at a specific frequency corresponding to its qualities and functions.

The systematic chakra doctrine found its classical articulation in Tantric texts, particularly the Śat-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa (“Description of and Investigation into the Six Bodily Centres”) of Pūrṇānanda (c. 16th century CE), translated and introduced to the Western world by the British scholar Sir John Woodroffe (Arthur Avalon) in his influential work The Serpent Power (1919). While this text gave the doctrine its classic form, the concept of subtle energy centres along the spinal axis is considerably older, appearing in various forms in earlier Tantric texts, in the Upanishads, and in the earliest Hatha Yoga literature.

Chakras are understood as intersections of nadis — the subtle channels (from Sanskrit nāḍī, meaning “tube” or “pipe”) through which prana (vital force) flows through the subtle body. Classical texts enumerate 72,000 or even 350,000 nadis, but the most important are three: the Suṣhumnā (the central channel, running from the base of the spine to the crown of the head through the centre of the spinal column), the Iḍā (the lunar nadi, running to the left of the Suṣhumnā, associated with the feminine principle, coolness, and the moon), and the Piṅgalā (the solar nadi, running to the right, associated with the masculine principle, heat, and the sun).

The major chakras are located at the points where the Iḍā and Piṅgalā nadis cross the Suṣhumnā, creating vortices of intensified subtle energy. Each chakra governs specific aspects of human experience — physical, psychological, and spiritual — and each represents a level of consciousness or a stage in the soul’s evolution from dense matter to pure spirit. The seven-chakra model (which became dominant in Western representations through the Theosophical tradition) maps a complete hierarchy of human experience from the most basic survival instincts of the root chakra to the pure, non-dual awareness of the crown.

The relationship between chakras and prana is fundamental. Prana is not simply breath (though breath is its primary vehicle in the physical body) but the universal life-force that animates all living beings and, in Tantric cosmology, is ultimately identical with Shakti — the divine feminine creative energy that is the active, dynamic aspect of the ultimate divine reality. The practice of pranayama (breath regulation and control) in Hatha Yoga works directly with prana to cleanse the nadis and balance the chakras, preparing the subtle body for the deeper practices of meditation and Kundalini awakening.

The colours, seed mantras (bīja), deities, animals, and geometric forms associated with each chakra in the classical texts are not arbitrary symbols but precisely specified yantra and mantra — visual and sonic forms that embody and evoke the specific quality of each chakra’s energy. Meditating on these forms, visualising the associated colours and geometry, and vibrating the seed mantras are understood to activate, balance, and open the corresponding chakras.

The Seven-Chakra System

Muladhara

Root Chakra — Base of Spine

Element: Earth. Colour: Red. Seed mantra: LAM. Deity: Brahma. Petals: 4. The foundation chakra of physical existence, survival instincts, security, and the connection to the earth. The resting place of the dormant Kundalini Shakti. When balanced: groundedness, vitality, and a sense of safety.

Svadhiṣṭhāna

Sacral Chakra — Below Navel

Element: Water. Colour: Orange. Seed mantra: VAM. Deity: Vishnu. Petals: 6. The centre of sexuality, creativity, pleasure, and fluid, adaptive emotion. Governs the reproductive organs and the experience of sensual delight. When balanced: creative vitality, emotional fluidity, and healthy relationships.

Maṇipūra

Solar Plexus — Navel Area

Element: Fire. Colour: Yellow. Seed mantra: RAM. Deity: Rudra. Petals: 10. The “city of jewels” — the seat of personal will, self-esteem, digestion (literal and psychological), and transformative fire. When balanced: personal power, healthy assertiveness, and purposeful action.

Anāhata

Heart Chakra — Heart Centre

Element: Air. Colour: Green. Seed mantra: YAM. Deity: Isha. Petals: 12. The “unstruck sound” — the pivotal centre of unconditional love, compassion, and the integration of the lower and higher chakras. When balanced: empathy, genuine love, forgiveness, and the capacity for spiritual devotion.

Viśuddha

Throat Chakra — Throat

Element: Ether/Space. Colour: Blue. Seed mantra: HAM. Deity: Sadasiva. Petals: 16. The centre of communication, authentic self-expression, creativity, and the resonant power of the spoken word. When balanced: clear, truthful expression, the ability to listen deeply, and creative articulacy.

Ājñā

Third Eye — Between Eyebrows

Element: Light/Mind. Colour: Indigo. Seed mantra: OM. Deity: Ardhanarishvara. Petals: 2 (or 96). The seat of intuition, clairvoyance, and direct spiritual perception beyond ordinary sensory reality. When balanced: clarity of insight, the ability to perceive subtle realities, and access to inner wisdom.

Sahasṛāra

Crown Chakra — Crown of Head

Element: Pure Consciousness. Colour: Violet/White. Seed mantra: Silence or AUM. Petals: 1,000 (symbolic of infinite). The thousand-petalled lotus — the point of union between individual consciousness and universal consciousness, between Kundalini Shakti and Shiva. Full activation constitutes samadhi — non-dual realisation.

Kundalini: The Serpent Power

Kundalini (Sanskrit: kuṇḍalinī — “coiled one”) is the latent divine energy that lies dormant in most human beings, coiled three and a half times around the Svayambhu Linga (a symbol of Shiva) at the Muladhara chakra at the base of the spine. In Tantric cosmology, Kundalini is identified with Shakti herself — the primordial divine feminine energy, the creative power of the universe, who has descended into matter and taken up her temporary dwelling in the most basic chakra of the individual human body. Her ascent through the chakras to the crown represents the return of the divine feminine to union with the divine masculine (Shiva) at the highest point of consciousness.

The process of Kundalini awakening begins when, through grace, intense spiritual practice, or sometimes spontaneously, the sleeping Kundalini is aroused. She rises through the central channel (Suṣhumnā), piercing each chakra in succession. As she passes through each chakra, the psychological and spiritual qualities associated with that chakra are intensified, purified, and eventually transcended. The experience can be blissful or overwhelming, depending on the state of the subtle body’s preparation: where the nadis are clear and the chakras balanced, the rising Kundalini brings progressive illumination; where there are blockages, the encounter with the Kundalini energy can be disturbing or even destabilising.

When Kundalini reaches the Sahasrara chakra at the crown, the union of Shakti (dynamic divine energy) and Shiva (pure consciousness) is complete. The individual self merges into the universal Self; the separate wave recognises itself as the ocean. This is samadhi in its highest form — nirvakalpa samadhi, the state without modifications, the direct realisation of the non-dual nature of reality. The adept who has achieved this union can live in the world with the full knowledge that it is nothing other than Shiva-Shakti at play — the cosmic dance of Consciousness and Energy in perpetual, joyful self-creation.

The dangers of premature or unguided Kundalini awakening are well attested in the tradition and increasingly recognised in contemporary therapeutic contexts. The American psychiatrist Lee Sannella’s The Kundalini Experience (1976) and Stanislav Grof’s work on “spiritual emergency” have documented cases of spontaneous Kundalini awakening presenting as psychiatric crisis, and emphasise the importance of qualified guidance and gradual preparation for the safe passage of this energy.

Variations in Different Traditions

Tibetan Buddhist Chakra Systems

Tibetan Buddhist Tantra recognises a different and smaller number of primary chakras — typically four (in some systems) or five (in most Highest Yoga Tantra systems). The Tibetan system emphasises the central channel (avadhuti) and the two flanking channels (roma and kyangma), with the major chakras at the crown, throat, heart, navel, and (in some systems) the secret place. The Tibetan system is particularly associated with the practices of Tummo (inner heat), the yoga of the dream state, and the yoga of the clear light, which work with the central channel and its chakras to dissolve ordinary consciousness into the Ground Luminosity.

Chinese Dantian System

Chinese Taoist and Chinese medical traditions recognise three primary energy centres called dantian (丹田 — “fields of elixir”): the lower dantian (below the navel — the seat of jing, vital essence, and physical vitality), the middle dantian (at the heart — the seat of qi, vital force, and emotional life), and the upper dantian (between the eyebrows — the seat of shen, spirit and spiritual consciousness). Taoist inner alchemy circulates and refines these three treasures through the microcosmic orbit, drawing energy up the spine and down the front of the body in a continuous circuit of refinement.

Sufi Lataif

Sufi mysticism has its own system of subtle energy centres called lataif (singular: latifa — Arabic for “subtle one”). The Naqshbandi order works with five lataif located in the chest (qalb — heart; ruh — spirit; sirr — secret; khafi — hidden; akhfa — most hidden), each associated with a colour, a prophet, and a specific mode of divine consciousness. The Sufi lataif are activated through the remembrance of God (dhikr) and the transmission of the master’s blessing (baraka) to the disciple’s subtle body.

The Chakras as Cosmological Map

The chakric system is not merely a model of individual anatomy but a cosmological map in miniature. Each chakra corresponds to a level of cosmic manifestation, a world or plane of existence in the great hierarchy of being. The Muladhara (Earth) corresponds to the physical world; the Svadhisthana (Water) to the vital-astral world; the Manipura (Fire) to the lower mental; the Anahata (Air) to the higher mental and causal; the Vishuddha (Ether) to the buddhic or intuitional plane; the Ajna (Light/Mind) to the monadic or atmic; and the Sahasrara (Pure Consciousness) to the Adi or Absolute.

In this reading, the human body becomes a vertical axis mundi — a pillar connecting the earthly and the heavenly, with each chakra serving as a gateway to a corresponding cosmic dimension. The ascent of Kundalini through the chakras is simultaneously the soul’s ascent through the cosmic planes, a recapitulation within the individual body of the great journey from matter to spirit that is the destiny of all consciousness. The body is not the prison of the soul but the vehicle of its liberation — the very instrument through which the divine consciousness that has descended into matter turns and begins its return to the source.

The macrocosm–microcosm principle applies here with full force: the human being is not merely in the cosmos but is the cosmos in miniature. To know the chakras is to know the architecture of reality; to work with the chakras is to participate consciously in the cosmic process of divine self-manifestation and return. The Hermetic insight “As above, so below” receives its most intimate confirmation in the chakric system: the entire ladder of being, from earth to the Absolute, is inscribed within the human subtle body.

Chakras in Esoteric Practice

Meditation & Visualisation

Classical Tantric chakra meditation involves focusing attention on each chakra in sequence, visualising its lotus form, colour, presiding deity, and seed mantra. This dharana (concentration) practice progressively purifies and awakens each centre, developing the specific quality of consciousness associated with it. The Theosophical and Western esoteric traditions developed their own chakra meditation practices drawing on these classical sources but adapting them for Western practitioners.

Breathwork & Pranayama

Pranayama — the yogic science of breath regulation — works directly with the prana flowing through the nadis to cleanse blockages, balance the ida and pingala channels, and direct energy into the Sushumna for its upward journey. Specific pranayama techniques such as Nadi Shodhana (alternate nostril breathing), Bhastrika (bellows breath), and Kumbhaka (breath retention) are prescribed for specific effects on the chakric system.

Sound Healing & Mantra

Each chakra has its bija mantra (seed sound) — a single syllable that embodies and evokes the vibratory quality of that chakra: LAM, VAM, RAM, YAM, HAM, OM, and silence. Chanting these mantras, either aloud or internally, is understood to resonate with and activate the corresponding chakra. Contemporary sound healing uses singing bowls, tuning forks, and vocal toning tuned to the frequencies associated with each chakra for therapeutic and spiritual purposes.
