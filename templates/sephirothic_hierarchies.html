Sephirothic Hierarchies

The divine architecture of emanated being — from the ineffable Crown to the Kingdom of earth, the Sephiroth as grades of divine presence in all worlds.

The Sephiroth as Hierarchy

When the Tree of Life is read vertically — from Crown to Kingdom, from Kether to Malkuth — it reveals itself as a hierarchy of divine self-disclosure, each level more specific, more limited, and more accessible than the level above it, yet each genuinely participating in the divine life that flows through the entire structure from its source in the infinite Ein Sof. The hierarchy is not a chain of diminishing value but a graded series of divine expressions, each perfect in its own way, each necessary to the completeness of the whole.

The three highest Sephiroth — Kether (Crown), Chokmah (Wisdom), and Binah (Understanding) — form the Supernal Triangle, the realm of pure divine being beyond ordinary human comprehension. These three are so transcendent, so close to the Ein Sof, that the Kabbalistic tradition describes a “Veil of the Abyss” (Paroketh) between the Supernals and the lower seven Sephiroth, and a further “hidden Sephirah” or pseudo-Sephirah — Daath (Knowledge) — at the point where the Abyss is bridged. Daath is not a full Sephirah but the point of intersection of the supernal energies, sometimes described as the throat or voice of the Tree.

The middle six Sephiroth — Chesed through Yesod — form the lower part of the Tree. They are grouped by Kabbalistic commentators into an Ethical Triangle (Chesed, Geburah, Tiphareth) and an Astral Triangle (Netzach, Hod, Yesod). Tiphareth, at the centre of the entire Tree and at the heart of the Ethical Triangle, is the most harmonious and integrative Sephirah — the mediating principle that balances Mercy and Severity, above and below, and that in Christian Kabbalistic interpretation is associated with the Christ as the Mediator between God and humanity.

Malkuth (Kingdom) occupies the lowest position on the Tree — yet it is by no means the least important. Malkuth is the throne of the Shekinah, the divine presence that dwells within the material world. The entire purpose of the divine emanation is to bring the divine light all the way down into the most dense and specific level of existence — to sanctify the material world by making it a transparent vehicle of the divine. In this reading, Malkuth is the fulfilment of the creative process, not its degradation.

The concept of grades of divine self-disclosure is the key to understanding the Sephirothic hierarchy. Ein Sof (the Infinite) is entirely beyond description or predication; Kether is the first and most transcendent description, merely acknowledging that the Infinite has a “Crown” or point of contact with the created order; Chokmah and Binah introduce the first differentiations — wisdom and understanding, force and form. As the hierarchy descends, the divine qualities become progressively more specific, more differentiated, more apprehensible, and ultimately more manifest in the physical world. Each Sephirah is a facet of the divine being polished to reflect the Infinite Light in a specific colour and quality.

The Lurianic doctrine of Shevirat haKelim (the Breaking of the Vessels) adds a catastrophic dimension to this hierarchy: in the process of creation, the divine light flowing down the Tree was too intense for the lower Sephiroth (from Chesed downward) to contain, and their vessels shattered, scattering holy sparks (nitzotzot) throughout the void of chaos. This cosmic catastrophe explains the presence of evil, suffering, and concealment in the world — the broken shards (Qliphoth) of the shattered vessels now serve as “husks” or “shells” imprisoning the scattered sparks of divine light that are the souls of all beings. The work of Tikkun (repair) — the restoration of the cosmic order — involves gathering these sparks back into their proper Sephirothic vessels through religious and ethical action.

Angels & Archangels of the Sephiroth

Kether

Archangel: Metatron

Angel choir: Chayot HaKodesh (Holy Living Creatures). Metatron is the “Prince of the Face” or “Lesser YHVH”, the highest of the archangels and the scribe of heaven. In some traditions, Metatron is the transformed form of the patriarch Enoch, who “walked with God and was no more.”

Chokmah

Archangel: Raziel

Angel choir: Ophanim (Wheels). Raziel is the “Secret of God” — the Archangel of divine mysteries and hidden knowledge, said to have given to Adam the Sefer Raziel HaMalakh (Book of the Angel Raziel) containing the wisdom of creation. He stands at the edge of heaven, hearing the divine decrees and recording them.

Binah

Archangel: Tzaphkiel

Angel choir: Aralim (Thrones). Tzaphkiel is the “Contemplation of God” — the Archangel of divine contemplation and the deep, formative understanding associated with Binah. The Aralim are the angels of divine majesty and the seat of God’s justice in its most elevated and compassionate form.

Chesed

Archangel: Tzadkiel

Angel choir: Chasmalim (Brilliant Ones). Tzadkiel is the “Righteousness of God” — the Archangel of divine mercy, forgiveness, and justice. He is said to have restrained Abraham’s hand on Mount Moriah. The Chasmalim surround the divine throne in radiant, golden glory.

Geburah

Archangel: Khamael

Angel choir: Seraphim (Burning Ones). Khamael (or Camael) is the “Severity of God” — the Archangel of divine strength, judgment, and the courage to face what must be faced. The Seraphim of Isaiah’s vision cry “Holy, holy, holy” and burn with the intensity of divine love that purifies and perfects.

Tiphareth

Archangel: Raphael

Angel choir: Malachim (Kings or Angels). Raphael is the “Healing of God” — the Archangel of healing, beauty, and the solar heart of the Tree. In the Book of Tobit he appears as a companion and guide. Some systems place Michael here instead. The Malachim are the messengers and emissaries of divine beauty and harmony.

Netzach

Archangel: Haniel

Angel choir: Elohim (Gods). Haniel (or Anael) is the “Grace of God” — the Archangel of divine love, beauty, and the ecstatic joy of existence. Associated with Venus and the sphere of emotion and desire in its highest form. The Elohim carry the divine qualities of Netzach into all levels of creation.

Hod

Archangel: Michael

Angel choir: Bene Elohim (Sons of God). Michael is “Who Is Like God” — the warrior-archangel, the protector, the bearer of divine splendour in its communicative, active form. Associated with Mercury and the intellect’s capacity for magical operation and divine communication. Some systems place Raphael here.

Yesod

Archangel: Gabriel

Angel choir: Kerubim (Cherubim). Gabriel is the “Strength of God” — the Archangel of divine messages, dreams, and the lunar sphere. He announced the birth of Jesus and Mohammed’s prophetic calling. The Kerubim guard the thresholds between worlds, embodying the power of the astral foundation.

Malkuth

Archangel: Sandalphon

Angel choir: Ishim (Souls of Fire or Men). Sandalphon, twin of Metatron, is the Archangel of the Earth — the grounding, embodying divine presence in the material world. He is said to weave the prayers of the faithful into garlands for God. The Ishim are the souls of the righteous who have been perfected and serve as angels in the earthly sphere.

Divine Names at Each Sephirah

Each Sephirah is associated with one of the divine Names of God in the Hebrew Bible, each Name revealing a different aspect of the divine nature. These Names are not merely labels but, in Kabbalistic theology, constitutive aspects of God — the ways in which the Ein Sof makes itself knowable to creation. The contemplation and vibration of these Names in meditation and ceremonial practice is understood to attune the practitioner to the specific quality of the divine that each Name embodies.

Kether: Ehyeh (אהיה)

“I Am” or “I Am That I Am” — the Name revealed to Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:14). Pure being, the first and most fundamental self-designation of the divine. Ehyeh suggests absolute, self-sufficient existence.

Chokmah: Yah (יה)

The abbreviated form of the Tetragrammaton, associated with the first flash of divine creative wisdom. Found in the Hebrew word Hallelujah (“Praise Yah”). Two letters, suggesting the primordial duality of wisdom.

Binah: YHVH Elohim

The combination of the masculine Tetragrammaton and the plural feminine Elohim — the creative divine Name of Genesis 2, the formative, world-creating aspect of God. The conjunction of force and form in the supreme Mother.

Chesed: El (אל)

“God” in the most general sense — the mighty, powerful, but also merciful God. El is the ancient Semitic generic term for divinity, associated with the Canaanite high god El and with strength, benevolence, and the paternal divine quality.

Geburah: Elohim Gibor

“Mighty God” — the Name of divine power, strength, and severity in its most concentrated form. Elohim Gibor is the God of battles, of irresistible force, of purifying fire. His justice is absolute and his power cannot be gainsaid.

Tiphareth: YHVH Eloah ve-Da'at

“YHVH, God of Knowledge” — the Name of the solar heart-centre, combining the transcendent Tetragrammaton with the intimacy of knowledge (Da'at). The Name of the reconciler, the mediating principle that makes the divine knowable and the human divine.

Netzach: YHVH Tzabaoth

“YHVH of Hosts” — the great commanding Name of the divine as the Lord of the celestial armies. Associated with victory, the natural forces, and the divine vitality that expresses itself through desire and love in all their forms.

Hod: Elohim Tzabaoth

“God of Hosts” in the Elohim form — the God of the concrete intellectual and magical operations, the Name associated with the splendour of divine intelligence as it expresses itself in the multiplicity of forms and communications.

Yesod: Shaddai El Chai

“Almighty Living God” — El Shaddai is one of the most ancient Hebrew divine Names, associated with power and nourishment (possibly from “breasts” or “mountains”). Chai (“living”) emphasises the vital, life-giving quality of the lunar foundation.

Malkuth: Adonai Melech

“Lord, King” — the Name of the divine as immanent sovereign of the material world, the Shekinah’s Name. Adonai (“my Lord”) is the Name spoken aloud in place of the ineffable Tetragrammaton, making it the most accessible and intimately personal of all the divine Names.

The Qliphoth: Shadow Hierarchies

What Are the Qliphoth?

The Qliphoth (קליפות — “shells” or “husks”, singular: Qliphah) are the shadow or demonic counterpart of each Sephirah — the result of the Sephirah’s energy operating without the balancing influence of its partner or without connection to the divine source. In the Lurianic system, the Qliphoth are the shards of the broken vessels from the Shevirat haKelim, the broken vessels that now imprison fragments of divine light and constitute the domain of evil, impurity, and spiritual opacity. The Tree of Death or Etz HaDa'at is the inverse mirror of the Tree of Life.

The Qliphothic Hierarchy

Each Sephirah has its corresponding Qliphah: Kether’s shadow is the void of formless chaos; Chokmah’s is chaotic force without wisdom; Binah’s is Sorrow (excessive restriction); Chesed’s is blind, destructive mercy; Geburah’s is tyranny; Tiphareth’s is the great adversary (Sorath); Netzach’s is lust; Hod’s is falsehood; Yesod’s is mechanicality and illusion; Malkuth’s is the “Blind One” or putrefaction. The demonic hierarchies — from Lilith at Malkuth’s shadow to the great nameless void at Kether’s inverse — constitute the Nightside of Eden described in esoteric tradition.

The Sephirothic Hierarchy in the Four Worlds

One of the most powerful aspects of Kabbalistic cosmology is the concept that the complete Tree of Life — with all ten Sephiroth — manifests at each of the Four Worlds (Atziluth, Beriah, Yetzirah, Assiah). This creates a fourfold structure of ten, producing a 40-fold hierarchy of divine manifestation in which every level of every Sephirah can be precisely located. Furthermore, as noted in the overview of the Tree of Life, the Malkuth of one world becomes the Kether of the world below it, so that the four worlds interlock in a continuous chain of emanation.

In Atziluth (the world of divine emanation), the Sephiroth exist as the divine Names — the ten aspects of the divine being in their most transcendent, least differentiated form. In Beriah (the world of creation), they manifest as the Archangels — the great archetypal intelligences through whom the divine plan is conceived and directed. In Yetzirah (the world of formation), they manifest as the choirs of angels — the executing powers through whom the divine plan is carried into the astral fabric. In Assiah (the world of action), they manifest as the planets, elements, and physical structures of the material cosmos.

The practical implication of this 40-fold structure is that any spiritual operation — any invocation, meditation, or ritual — can be directed to the specific level of a specific Sephirah at which the desired quality of consciousness operates. A ritual working with divine Names operates at the Atziluthic level; one working with Archangelic imagery operates at the Briatic level; one working with angelic forces operates at the Yetziratic level; one working with physical correspondences operates at the Assiatic level. The skilled Kabbalist navigates all four levels simultaneously, working consciously across the entire hierarchy.

Practical & Contemplative Approaches

Pathworking the Sephiroth

Pathworking is the practice of undertaking guided or self-directed meditative journeys through the Paths and Sephiroth of the Tree of Life, encountering the beings, symbols, and qualities associated with each. A Sephirothic pathworking typically involves entering a meditative state, visualising oneself approaching a specific Sephirah through its associated symbolic landscape, and engaging with the archangel, divine Name, and qualities of that sphere. Over time, the practitioner builds an inner relationship with each Sephirah as a living dimension of experience.

Hermetic Kabbalah & Ritual

In the Western ceremonial magic tradition (most fully systematised by the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and its successors), the Sephiroth are the objects of formal ritual invocation. The Lesser and Greater Banishing Rituals, the Hexagram rituals, and the Rose Cross ritual all work with Sephirothic and Pathworking energies. The Grade system of the Golden Dawn corresponded the degrees of initiation to the ascent of the Tree, each grade associated with a specific Sephirah and its qualities, so that initiation was understood as literal progress up the Sephirothic hierarchy.

Sephirothic Meditation

Jewish Kabbalistic practise includes meditative techniques for ascending through the Sephiroth in devekut (cleaving to God) — the contemplative absorption of the individual soul in the divine quality of each Sephirah. The hitbonenut meditation of Chabad Hasidism involves sustained, discursive contemplation of a divine concept until it is thoroughly known by the mind; this then gives rise to hitpa'alut (emotional arousal) and eventually to bitul (self-nullification) — the dissolution of the ego-sense in the divine Nothingness of Kether or Ein Sof.
