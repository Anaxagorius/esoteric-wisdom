David Fravor

"The Tic-Tac Pilot" · b. 1964

Brief Biography

Commander David Fravor (USN, Ret.) is a career naval aviator with over 3,000 flight hours in the F/A-18 and extensive combat experience including operations over Iraq during Operations Desert Storm and Iraqi Freedom. He served as Commanding Officer of the VFA-41 "Black Aces" squadron aboard USS Nimitz, one of the most coveted leadership positions in carrier aviation. He is by every professional measure an exceptional and experienced aviator — exactly the kind of witness whose credibility is highest in evaluating unusual aerial phenomena.

On 14 November 2004, Fravor had the primary visual intercept of the "Tic-Tac" UAP — the object that produced the FLIR1 infrared video later officially released by the DoD. He remained largely private about his experience until the 2017 New York Times story, after which he gave dozens of interviews and testified before Congress. He runs a private aviation company and has been outspoken in insisting that what he encountered was beyond any known human technology.

The Tic-Tac Encounter

Fravor's encounter is documented in detail on this site's Nimitz Encounter page. The key elements: descent from 20,000 feet to observe a churning disturbance on the ocean surface; visual acquisition of a white, elongated, oblong object — "like a Tic-Tac breath mint" — approximately 40 feet long with no visible wings, control surfaces, or propulsion system; an apparent awareness response from the object; a mirror-manoeuvre that suggested intelligent, reactive behaviour; and an instantaneous departure that rendered pursuit impossible.

What makes Fravor's testimony particularly significant in the current disclosure context is not merely the sighting itself but his categorical assessment of it. He has consistently and publicly stated: "I can tell you I think it was not from this world" and "I'm not crazy, haven't drunk the Kool-Aid... what I saw, with my own eyes, was not from this Earth." This assessment, from an active-duty career military officer of the highest professional standing, speaking on record in the New York Times and subsequently under congressional oath, represented a qualitative shift in the public discourse around UAP.

Congressional Testimony

Fravor testified publicly before the House Armed Services Committee and the House Intelligence Committee, providing a calm, technically precise account of his encounter and fielding questions from members of Congress with the same no-nonsense directness he brings to all public appearances. His testimony is available in the congressional record and stands as one of the most credible first-hand accounts of a UAP encounter ever given in an official government setting.

Fravor has been consistent in his view that the phenomenon warrants serious government investigation and that the institutional resistance to taking it seriously represents a failure of national security policy. He has also been notably sceptical of speculative claims — the alien abduction narrative, contactee accounts — and focuses strictly on what he personally observed and what the sensor data shows, which he regards as sufficient in itself to demand explanation.

"I want to fly. I want to go find that thing. And what I found was something that I could not explain."
— Commander David Fravor (USN, Ret.)
