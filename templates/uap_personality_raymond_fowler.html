Raymond E. Fowler

"Chronicler of Contact" · 1933–2021

Brief Biography

Raymond E. Fowler was born on 6 August 1933 in Salem, Massachusetts. He worked as a senior systems analyst at GTE Government Systems (formerly Sylvania's Electronic Defense Laboratories) — a defence contractor involved in missile guidance and electronic warfare systems. This technical background gave his investigative work an analytical rigour absent from many civilian UFO researchers of his era. He was also deeply religious — a deacon in his evangelical Protestant church — and this faith shaped both his personal interpretation of his findings and his ability to connect with many witnesses who shared it.

Fowler served as Section Director for Massachusetts under NICAP (National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena) from 1963 and became a prominent figure in MUFON, for which he investigated hundreds of cases. He investigated the legendary Betty Andreasson case over nearly two decades, producing five books on it alone. He died on 15 October 2021.

The Betty Andreasson Case

Fowler's defining investigative work is the Betty Andreasson Luca case — one of the most thoroughly documented and unusual abduction accounts in the literature. Betty, a devout Christian from South Ashburnham, Massachusetts, reported a series of contacts beginning in 1967, investigated by Fowler through extensive regressive hypnosis sessions that produced a detailed and internally consistent narrative of repeated contact with beings she associated with a spiritual light.

What distinguished the Andreasson case from other abduction reports was its extraordinary depth: hundreds of hours of hypnotic session recordings, consistent narrative across multiple sessions conducted by different investigators, corroborating accounts from family members, and specific details — particularly the "One" encounter in which Betty described meeting a vast luminous intelligence — that seemed to transcend the standard abduction script. Fowler was initially cautious about religious interpretations but eventually concluded that the contacts had a genuine spiritual dimension that conventional ET hypotheses could not fully accommodate.

He documented the case across five books: The Andreasson Affair (1979), The Andreasson Affair: Phase Two (1982), The Watchers (1990), The Watchers II (1995), and The Andreasson Legacy (1997). These books collectively represent one of the most detailed individual contact case archives in the history of UFO research.

Broader Investigative Work

Beyond the Andreasson case, Fowler investigated hundreds of New England UFO incidents, contributed to the ground-breaking Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects (the Condon Report, 1969) as a dissenting voice, and maintained systematic case files that he contributed to both NICAP and MUFON archives. His autobiography, UFO Testament (2002), documented his own anomalous personal experiences and his eventual discovery that he himself had been subject to contact events throughout his life — a disclosure that recontextualised his lifelong investigative obsession as something more personal than professional detachment had implied.
