As Above, So Below

The Hermetic axiom from the Emerald Tablet of Hermes Trismegistus — the declaration that celestial and terrestrial realities mirror one another in a universal correspondence that the wise can read, navigate, and ultimately employ in the great work of transformation.

What Is "As Above, So Below"?

The Emerald Tablet — Tabula Smaragdina in Latin, purportedly inscribed on an emerald slab found in the tomb of Hermes Trismegistus — is among the most influential texts in the entire Western esoteric tradition, despite (or because of) its extraordinary brevity. Its earliest known version appears in Arabic, in a text attributed to Apollonius of Tyana and translated into Latin in the early medieval period; it was subsequently incorporated into alchemical literature and became one of the foundational texts of the whole Hermetic tradition. The central axiom — "That which is above is like that which is below, and that which is below is like that which is above, to accomplish the miracles of the One Thing" (Quod est inferius est sicut quod est superius, et quod est superius est sicut quod est inferius, ad perpetranda miracula rei unius) — is simultaneously a cosmological claim, an epistemological method, and an operational programme. It declares that the structure of the higher realms is replicated in the structure of the lower, and that this correspondence is not merely contemplative but practically applicable: knowing the correspondence, the wise can work in the lower realm in ways that produce effects in the higher, and vice versa.

The full context of the axiom is crucial and frequently overlooked. The Emerald Tablet does not merely assert a formal similarity between levels of reality; it grounds this similarity in the derivation of all things from a single source — the "One Thing" (Latin: una res) that is simultaneously the goal of the alchemical work and the origin from which all multiplicity flows. The correspondence between above and below is not coincidental or merely analogical but ontological: the lower realities are genuine expressions of the higher, genuine participations in the same principles, not mere copies of external models. This ontological grounding distinguishes the Hermetic principle from mere symbolic parallelism: it means that working with lower realities that genuinely participate in higher ones is actually working with the higher ones, just at a different level of manifestation. The magician, the astrologer, and the alchemist are all working with the same principles — the same "One Thing" — just at different points in its expression from above to below.

Neoplatonic emanation theory provides the most rigorous philosophical foundation for the as-above-so-below principle. In Plotinus's account, the One — the absolutely simple, undifferentiated source of all reality — overflows its own fullness in a necessary act of productive emanation, giving rise to Nous (Intellect), which in turn gives rise to Psyche (Soul), which in turn gives rise to the material world. Each successive level of emanation is less concentrated, less unified, and less complete than the level above it, but it genuinely participates in the level from which it flows: the material world genuinely participates in Soul, Soul genuinely participates in Intellect, and Intellect genuinely participates in the One. The great Neoplatonist metaphors of emanation — the sun and its light, the spring and the river flowing from it — all illustrate the same point: the lower retains the nature and structure of the higher even while descending to a more dilute or dispersed expression of it. This is why the correspondence between levels is a correspondence of structure and principle rather than mere surface similarity.

The operative application of as-above-so-below in the tradition of sympathetic magic rests on the conviction that like affects like across levels of reality: that working with the symbol or material correspondent of a higher principle is not merely a representation of working with that principle but is actually working with it, through the genuine participation of the lower in the higher. The Neoplatonist Iamblichus, in his De Mysteriis (On the Mysteries), argues that the gods have placed divine traces and symbols throughout the material world, and that theurgical ritual — working with these divine traces in appropriate combinations and sequences — genuinely activates divine power, because the divine traces genuinely participate in the divinity they represent. The magician who works with solar herbs, solar metals, solar colours, and solar musical notes in a ritual honouring the sun is not engaging in a merely symbolic activity but is genuinely summoning and concentrating solar power, because each of these material items genuinely participates in the solar principle and carries its operative virtue.

Astrology is the archetypal science of as-above-so-below: the entire enterprise of astrological interpretation rests on the conviction that the celestial configurations at the moment of birth (above) genuinely correspond to the individual's psychological and physical constitution (below), not because the planets causally impose their qualities on the newborn but because both the celestial configurations and the individual's constitution are expressions of the same cosmic moment — two faces of a single universal pattern that manifests simultaneously at all levels of reality. This non-causal correspondence — what C.G. Jung would later call "synchronicity," the acausal meaningful coincidence of inner and outer events — is the operative principle of all astrological practice. The planets do not cause the character; both the planets and the character express the same underlying cosmic pattern. The astrologer reads the above in order to understand the below, and equally could read the below in order to understand the above, because they are, at the level of principle, the same thing seen from different vantage points.

Alchemy as a spiritual practice — as opposed to or in addition to its physical operations in the laboratory — represents the most concentrated working-out of the as-above-so-below principle. The alchemist works with physical materials in the laboratory (below), but the conviction is that this physical work genuinely corresponds to and participates in an inner spiritual work (above): as the prima materia is dissolved, purified, and reconstituted in the laboratory, so the alchemist's own soul is dissolving, purifying, and reconstituting itself. The physical operations are not merely symbolic illustrations of a spiritual process that is happening elsewhere; they are genuine participations in the spiritual process through the mechanism of correspondence. The Paracelsian tradition makes this explicit: the alchemist who performs the work without simultaneously undergoing the inner transformation is producing nothing of spiritual value, however technically competent their laboratory practice. Conversely, the genuine spiritual alchemist — who undergoes the inner work of dissolution and reconstitution whether or not they work with physical materials — is performing the genuine Great Work regardless of their laboratory activity.

Theurgy — the ritual practice of ascent to the divine through material means — represents perhaps the most philosophically sophisticated application of the as-above-so-below principle. The theurgist, working within the Neoplatonic tradition, uses material objects — stones, herbs, animals, musical tones, incantations, geometric figures — that genuinely participate in and channel divine power, and combines them in structured ritual sequences designed to summon divine presences and raise the practitioner's own consciousness toward the divine. The philosophical justification is rigorous: if the material world genuinely participates in and reflects the divine world (above), then working systematically with the right material configurations is genuinely working with the divine, and can genuinely draw the divine downward into manifestation and draw the human practitioner upward toward the divine source. The boundary between above and below is not absolute but permeable, and properly structured ritual practice exploits this permeability in both directions.

The as-above-so-below principle has meaningful non-Western parallels that suggest it reflects a genuine structural insight about reality rather than merely a culturally specific metaphysical preference. In Buddhism, the Flower Garland (Avatamsaka) Sutra's image of Indra's Net — a vast celestial net in which each node holds a jewel that reflects all the others perfectly — expresses the same structural insight in a different metaphysical framework: each part of reality reflects the whole, and working with any part with proper understanding is working with the whole. The Taoist principle of the unity of Heaven and Earth — expressed in the paired trigrams of the I Ching, in which the patterns of cosmic change and the patterns of human life mirror and interpenetrate one another — is another non-Western articulation of the same fundamental conviction. The universality of this pattern across independent traditions is itself evidence, from the esoteric perspective, that it corresponds to something real about the structure of the universe that diverse contemplative cultures have independently discovered.

Key Concepts

📜

The Emerald Tablet

The most concise and influential statement of Hermetic philosophy — a brief text whose Arabic origins and Latin translations shaped the entire alchemical and magical tradition of the West, encapsulating in a few sentences a complete cosmological and operative programme.

🔄

Sympathetic Magic

The operative application of correspondence: like affects like across levels of reality. Working with the material correspondent of a higher principle is not merely symbolic but genuinely operative, because the material correspondent genuinely participates in the higher principle it represents.

🪐

Planetary Correspondences

The astrological system mapping celestial archetypes to terrestrial phenomena — herbs, metals, animals, colours, musical tones, body parts, character types — each planet expressing its principle simultaneously at all levels of the great chain of being.

⬆️

Emanation

Neoplatonic theory: higher realities flow forth into lower, which genuinely participate in the higher and mirror its structure. Emanation provides the ontological grounding for correspondence: the lower reflects the higher not by convention but by genuine participation in it.

🕸️

Indra's Net

Buddhist metaphor from the Avatamsaka Sutra: each jewel in the divine net reflects all others — a non-Western formulation of total correspondence. Each part of reality perfectly mirrors the whole; working with any part with genuine understanding is working with the totality.

🔬

Alchemical Analogy

The work in the laboratory as mirror of the inner work: as the physical metals are transmuted through the alchemical operations, so the alchemist's soul is simultaneously transmuted through the same principles operating at the psychological and spiritual level.

Key Scholars

Julius Ruska

German orientalist and author of the first systematic scholarly study of the Emerald Tablet (1926). Ruska's philological research established the Arabic origins of the text and its subsequent transmission through Latin translations, correcting earlier myths about its Egyptian hieroglyphic origins.

Garth Fowden

British historian of late antiquity and author of The Egyptian Hermes (1986), the definitive account of the Hermetic tradition in its original Egyptian and Greco-Egyptian context. Fowden's work corrects the Renaissance myth of Hermes's extreme antiquity while illuminating the genuine depth and coherence of the Hermetic worldview.

Lyndy Abraham

British scholar of alchemical symbolism and author of A Dictionary of Alchemical Imagery (1998). Abraham's systematic mapping of alchemical symbols to their spiritual and psychological correspondences provides an invaluable guide to the as-above-so-below structure of alchemical symbolism.

Antoine Faivre

Pioneer of academic esotericism studies who identified "correspondences" as the first and most fundamental of his six characteristics of Western esotericism. For Faivre, the doctrine of universal correspondences — which the as-above-so-below axiom encapsulates — is the single most defining feature of the Western esoteric tradition.

"It is true, certain, and without falsehood, that whatever is below is like that which is above, to accomplish the miracles of one thing." — The Emerald Tablet
