Judaism (Original Jewish Religion)

The covenantal tradition of Israel, rooted in Torah, prophetic revelation, temple memory, and enduring communal law and practice.

Foundational Sources

Judaism is grounded in Torah and the Tanakh, with rabbinic interpretation preserved through Mishnah and Talmud. These texts shape law, ritual, theology, and communal identity.

✡️ Main Branches of Judaism

🔵 1. Orthodox Judaism

Most traditional mainstream branch, with strict adherence to Halakha and a view of Torah as divine and enduring.

  • Modern Orthodox: traditional observance with active engagement in modern education and professions.
  • Haredi (Ultra-Orthodox): highly conservative communities with strong rabbinic authority and communal boundaries.
  • Hasidic Judaism: mystical, dynasty-centered communities emphasizing devotion and spiritual joy.
  • Lithuanian / Yeshivish: non-Hasidic Haredi traditions centered on advanced Torah and Talmud study.

🟢 2. Conservative Judaism

A middle path between Orthodoxy and Reform, viewing Jewish law as binding yet historically adaptable.

🟡 3. Reform Judaism

The most liberal major branch, emphasizing ethical monotheism and personal religious autonomy over strict legal obligation.

🟣 4. Reconstructionist Judaism

Understands Judaism as an evolving religious civilization, with communities shaping practice in a democratic and progressive framework.

🟠 5. Renewal Judaism

Blends Jewish mysticism with contemporary spirituality, often emphasizing meditation, music, and inclusive worship.

🌍 Cultural / Ethnic Traditions

These are cultural-historical Jewish communities rather than separate religions, each with distinctive liturgical and communal customs.

  • 🟤 Ashkenazi Jews: historically rooted in Central and Eastern Europe; Yiddish language heritage.
  • 🟢 Sephardic Jews: origins in Iberia, later North Africa and the Middle East, with distinct liturgical traditions.
  • 🟠 Mizrahi Jews: Middle Eastern and related communities with rich regional ritual lineages.
  • ⚫ Ethiopian Jews (Beta Israel): unique long-standing historical practices and heritage.
  • 🔵 Other communities: Bene Israel, Cochin Jews, Bukharan Jews, and Mountain Jews.

🌿 Other Jewish Movements & Related Groups

  • 🔹 Karaite Judaism: rejects rabbinic oral law and follows the Hebrew Bible as sole authority.
  • 🔹 Humanistic Judaism: secular-humanist expression focused on Jewish culture and identity.
  • 🔹 Jewish Secularism / Cultural Judaism: Jewish identity outside regular religious observance.
  • 🔹 Samaritanism: an ancient related Israelite tradition, historically distinct from mainstream Judaism.

🧘 Spiritual & Mystical Traditions

Early mystical streams include Merkabah and Hekhalot traditions, later flowering into Kabbalah, where creation, emanation, and divine names are contemplated as pathways to sacred understanding.

🔮 Kabbalah: Jewish mysticism studied in different ways across multiple branches, with deep influence on Hasidic thought.

🔥 Hasidic dynasties: major dynastic lineages include Chabad-Lubavitch, Satmar, Breslov, Belz, and Ger (Gur), each with distinct leadership and devotional style.

Living Continuity

Jewish religious life remains active through prayer, Sabbath observance, festivals, study, ethical responsibility, and memory of covenantal history across generations and lands.
