Christianity

A world religion rooted in the life, teachings, death, and resurrection of Jesus of Nazareth — grounded in the scriptures of the Old and New Testaments and expressed across two millennia of theology, mysticism, and living devotion.

The Christian Tradition

Christianity emerged in first-century Judea from the ministry of Jesus of Nazareth, whom his followers identified as the promised Messiah (Christ) of the Hebrew scriptures. The faith spread rapidly through the Roman world, shaped by the missionary journeys of Paul of Tarsus and the witness of the earliest apostolic communities.

Its scriptural canon is divided into the Old Testament — the Hebrew Bible received through the Jewish tradition — and the New Testament, a collection of Gospels, letters, and apocalyptic writings produced by the early Christian communities in the first century CE.

The tradition encompasses vast theological and devotional diversity: Eastern Orthodoxy, Roman Catholicism, and the many branches of Protestantism each preserve distinct liturgical, doctrinal, and mystical emphases while sharing the core confession of Christ as Lord and Saviour.

Within Christianity lie rich streams of mystical and esoteric inquiry: Christian mysticism (Meister Eckhart, Hildegard of Bingen, John of the Cross), Christian Kabbalah (Pico della Mirandola, Johannes Reuchlin), Rosicrucianism, and Gnosticism — traditions that sought direct experience of the divine beyond the boundaries of institutional doctrine.

The King James Version of the Bible (1611) stands as one of the most influential literary and spiritual documents in the English-speaking world, shaping language, culture, and devotion across four centuries.

Today Christianity is the world's largest religion, with over two billion adherents across every continent — a living tradition of prayer, sacrament, scripture, community, and transformative encounter with the sacred.

Catholicism: Churches, Rites, and Movements

1) Latin (Roman) Catholic Church

The largest part of global Catholicism, historically associated with the Latin Rite.

  • Ordinary Form: the post–Vatican II Mass (Novus Ordo).
  • Extraordinary Form: the Traditional Latin Mass in approved contexts.

2) Eastern Catholic Churches (23 Churches)

Churches in full communion with Rome that preserve distinct liturgical and spiritual traditions.

  • Byzantine: Ukrainian Greek Catholic, Melkite, Ruthenian, Romanian Greek Catholic, Slovak, Hungarian, Italo-Albanian.
  • Alexandrian: Coptic Catholic, Ethiopian Catholic, Eritrean Catholic.
  • Antiochian (West Syrian): Maronite, Syriac Catholic, Syro-Malankara.
  • East Syrian (Chaldean): Chaldean Catholic, Syro-Malabar.
  • Armenian Rite: Armenian Catholic.

3) Distinct Catholic Traditions

  • Latin (Western) tradition
  • Byzantine tradition
  • Syriac traditions (East and West)
  • Coptic / Alexandrian tradition

4) Religious Orders and Spiritual Families

Orders are forms of life and spirituality within Catholicism rather than separate churches.

  • Benedictines
  • Franciscans
  • Dominicans
  • Jesuits
  • Carmelites
  • Augustinians

5) Movements and Styles of Practice

  • Charismatic Catholicism
  • Traditionalist Catholic currents
  • Liberation theology movements
  • Opus Dei
  • Focolare Movement
  • Neocatechumenal Way

6) Groups Not in Full Communion

Some groups identify as Catholic but are not fully integrated into Roman communion, including Old Catholic bodies, many independent Catholic churches, sedevacantist groups, and certain traditionalist splinters.

Main Historical Branches of Protestantism

Lutheranism

Founded by Martin Luther, emphasizing salvation by faith alone.

  • Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA)
  • Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod (LCMS)
  • Wisconsin Evangelical Lutheran Synod (WELS)

Reformed / Calvinist Tradition

Rooted in the teachings of John Calvin, emphasizing God’s sovereignty and predestination.

  • Presbyterian Church
  • Reformed Church in America
  • Dutch Reformed churches

Anglicanism

Began under Henry VIII, combining Catholic structure with Protestant theology.

  • Church of England
  • Episcopal Church (USA)
  • Anglican Communion worldwide

Anabaptist Movement

A Radical Reformation stream emphasizing adult baptism and separation from the state.

  • Mennonites
  • Amish
  • Hutterites

Evangelical & Revival Traditions

  • Baptist: believer’s baptism and local church autonomy (Southern Baptist Convention, American Baptist Churches).
  • Methodist / Wesleyan: founded by John Wesley with a focus on holiness and personal faith (United Methodist Church, AME Church, Wesleyan Church).
  • Holiness Movement: a Methodist offshoot emphasizing entire sanctification.
  • Pentecostalism: emphasis on the Holy Spirit and charismatic gifts (Assemblies of God, Church of God – Cleveland, Pentecostal Holiness Church).
  • Charismatic Movement: Pentecostal-style spirituality within other denominations.

Restorationist & Independent Movements

  • Restoration Movement: Churches of Christ, Christian Church (Disciples of Christ).
  • Adventist Movement: especially the Seventh-day Adventist Church.
  • Non-denominational Churches: independent congregations without formal affiliation.

Other Significant Protestant Families

  • Quakers (Religious Society of Friends)
  • Congregationalists
  • Salvation Army

Groups Sometimes Compared but Debated

Some groups are historically related to Protestantism but are not always classified as Protestant by scholars, including Jehovah’s Witnesses and some independent Bible churches.

🏝️ Main Mansions of Rastafari

🥁 1. Nyabinghi Order

Often regarded as the oldest and most traditional foundation of Rastafari identity and practice.

  • Strong focus on African identity and repatriation.
  • Groundations with traditional drumming and chanting.
  • Often strict lifestyle with Ital-oriented food disciplines.

👑 2. Bobo Shanti (EABIC)

Founded by Prince Emmanuel Charles Edwards and known for disciplined communal structure.

  • Emphasis on Black liberation and separation from “Babylon.”
  • Distinctive robes and turbans.
  • Formal gender roles and regular prayer routines.

✡️ 3. Twelve Tribes of Israel

Founded by Vernon Carrington (“Prophet Gad”) and often considered the most accessible mansion.

  • Members are associated with one of the twelve tribes of Israel.
  • Encourages Bible study and personal interpretation.
  • Generally more flexible in lifestyle and membership background.

🌿 Other Groups and Variations

  • House of Nyabinghi variations with local differences in leadership and ritual style.
  • Ethiopian World Federation–aligned Rastas with strong political and repatriation focus.
  • Modern or cultural Rastafari with flexible spiritual, musical, and heritage emphasis.
  • Independent Rastafari communities without formal mansion affiliation.

Differences across mansions are often seen in leadership structure, lifestyle rules, religious interpretation, and degree of organizational formality.
