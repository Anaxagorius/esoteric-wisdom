Christianity

A world religion rooted in the life, teachings, death, and resurrection of Jesus of Nazareth — grounded in the scriptures of the Old and New Testaments and expressed across two millennia of theology, mysticism, and living devotion.

The Christian Tradition

Christianity emerged in first-century Judea from the ministry of Jesus of Nazareth, whom his followers identified as the promised Messiah (Christ) of the Hebrew scriptures. The faith spread rapidly through the Roman world, shaped by the missionary journeys of Paul of Tarsus and the witness of the earliest apostolic communities.

Its scriptural canon is divided into the Old Testament — the Hebrew Bible received through the Jewish tradition — and the New Testament, a collection of Gospels, letters, and apocalyptic writings produced by the early Christian communities in the first century CE.

The tradition encompasses vast theological and devotional diversity: Eastern Orthodoxy, Roman Catholicism, and the many branches of Protestantism each preserve distinct liturgical, doctrinal, and mystical emphases while sharing the core confession of Christ as Lord and Saviour.

Within Christianity lie rich streams of mystical and esoteric inquiry: Christian mysticism (Meister Eckhart, Hildegard of Bingen, John of the Cross), Christian Kabbalah (Pico della Mirandola, Johannes Reuchlin), Rosicrucianism, and Gnosticism — traditions that sought direct experience of the divine beyond the boundaries of institutional doctrine.

The King James Version of the Bible (1611) stands as one of the most influential literary and spiritual documents in the English-speaking world, shaping language, culture, and devotion across four centuries.

Today Christianity is the world's largest religion, with over two billion adherents across every continent — a living tradition of prayer, sacrament, scripture, community, and transformative encounter with the sacred.
