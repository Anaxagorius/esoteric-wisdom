Dylan Borland

"Independent UAP Researcher & Analyst"

Brief Biography

Dylan Borland is an independent UAP researcher and data analyst who emerged within the open-source UAP research community during the post-2017 disclosure era. He is primarily known through his contributions to UAP-focused online communities, forums, and independent reporting platforms, where he has applied a systematic, data-driven methodology to case documentation and investigative analysis.

Borland represents a new generation of UAP researchers who have bypassed traditional institutional structures — MUFON, CUFOS, or government-adjacent groups — in favour of the decentralised, openly collaborative model made possible by the internet. His work emphasises transparency of methodology, primary source citation, and healthy scepticism toward both official narratives and unverified insider claims.

Research Approach and Contributions

Borland's approach to UAP research is characterised by rigorous source documentation, cross-referencing of government records (FOIA documents, congressional transcripts, DoD releases), and careful separation of verified facts from speculation. He has contributed to the mapping and analysis of UAP incident clusters, official programme histories, and the provenance of leaked or declassified materials.

His work within the online UAP community has helped establish standards for documentation and sourcing that have raised the overall quality of independent research. He has collaborated with other researchers in the distributed network of investigators who collectively serve as a kind of citizen intelligence community working in parallel to — and sometimes ahead of — mainstream journalism on UAP matters.

The New Model of UAP Research

Borland's career illustrates a significant structural shift in how UAP research is conducted and disseminated. The pre-internet model — dominated by a small number of authors, organisations, and conferences — has been replaced by a distributed network of independent researchers who collectively process and analyse the dramatically increased volume of publicly available UAP material generated by the post-2017 disclosure process. Researchers like Borland serve as connective tissue in this network: aggregating, verifying, and contextualising information for broader audiences who lack the time or background to process primary sources themselves.

The importance of this layer of independent analysis has grown as official UAP programmes have produced complex bureaucratic and legal outputs — classified programme structures, congressional testimony, inspector general reports, NDAA provisions — that require sustained, expert-level attention to interpret accurately. Borland's work contributes to building the informed public constituency that effective congressional oversight of UAP programmes ultimately requires.
