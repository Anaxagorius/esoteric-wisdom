Leslie Kean

"The Journalist Who Changed Everything" · b. 1957

Brief Biography

Leslie Kean is an American investigative journalist who has written for the Boston Globe, The Nation, International Herald Tribune, and many other publications on topics ranging from CIA activities in Burma to labour rights, before her career-defining work on UAP. Her approach to the subject has always been that of a methodologically rigorous journalist: demanding documentation, insisting on on-record sources, and refusing to speculate beyond what the evidence supports. This discipline — unusual in UAP coverage — gave her work an institutional credibility that made it impossible for mainstream editors to dismiss.

Her book UFOs: Generals, Pilots and Government Officials Go on Record (2010) was the first major UAP book to include only on-record testimony from credentialled individuals and was endorsed by figures including John Podesta, former White House chief of staff. It became a New York Times bestseller.

The 2017 New York Times Exposé

Kean's most historically significant work was her co-authorship of the December 2017 New York Times front-page story — co-bylined with journalists Ralph Blumenthal and Helene Cooper — revealing the existence of the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program, releasing two officially confirmed Navy UAP videos, and introducing Luis Elizondo and Harry Reid's role in funding the programme to the public. The article is widely credited with launching the current era of UAP disclosure — directly triggering the Pentagon's subsequent acknowledgment of the videos, the establishment of the UAP Task Force, and ultimately the congressional hearing process that produced David Grusch's testimony.

The story's placement on the front page of the New York Times — the world's most influential newspaper — was itself a signal that the UAP subject had cleared the credibility barrier that had confined it to fringe publications for seven decades. Kean's decade-long relationship building with insider sources, her methodological insistence on documentation, and her ability to navigate the Times' editorial process were all essential to this outcome.

Survival and the Paranormal

In 2013, Kean published Surviving Death: A Journalist Investigates Evidence for an Afterlife, applying the same evidential standards she brings to UAP to the question of consciousness survival — investigating near-death experiences, medium phenomena, and reincarnation research. The book reflects a broader view that the paranormal, including UAP, represents a cluster of genuine phenomena that science has failed to investigate adequately due to institutional scepticism rather than lack of evidence.

Kean continues to report on UAP for major publications and has been a central figure in the journalistic ecosystem that has brought the disclosure process to its current state.
