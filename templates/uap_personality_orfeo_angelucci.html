Orfeo Angelucci

"The Mystic of Burbank" · 1912–1993

Brief Biography

Orfeo Matthew Angelucci was born on 25 June 1912 in Trenton, New Jersey, into a working-class Italian-American family. He suffered chronic poor health from childhood — a condition he described as a hypersensitive nervous system — and this physical vulnerability, combined with an intensely introspective disposition, shaped his spiritual inclinations from an early age. He educated himself voraciously and harboured ambitions as a writer and philosopher.

During World War II, Angelucci worked in a defence plant, and by the late 1940s he was employed as a materials handler at Lockheed Aircraft Corporation's Burbank, California facility — one of the most sensitive aerospace manufacturing plants in the United States. He lived quietly in Burbank with his wife Mabel and their children. His coworkers described him as gentle, somewhat introverted, and prone to talking about unusual experiences.

His experiences culminated in the books The Secret of the Saucers (1955) and Son of the Sun (1959), which attracted a devoted following. Unlike some contactees, Angelucci consistently presented his experiences as simultaneously physical and mystical, resisting demands from UFO investigators to frame them purely as extraterrestrial encounters in the nuts-and-bolts sense.

The Contacts: A Journey into Inner Space

Angelucci's first encounter occurred on the night of 23 May 1952 as he was driving home from his Lockheed night shift along a stretch of Riverside Drive near the Los Angeles River. He noticed a glowing red oval in the sky that seemed to pace his car. Two smaller luminous discs then materialised and, to his astonishment, projected human voices directly into the interior of his car. The voices — calm, compassionate, and speaking perfect English — identified themselves as friends and told him they had observed him for some time.

Over subsequent months, Angelucci underwent a series of increasingly profound encounters. In one episode he described entering a translucent, dome-shaped craft and being shown images of Earth from space along with scenes of a paradisiacal civilisation. He met two beings he knew as Neptune and Lyra — extraordinarily beautiful humanoids radiating an almost painful sense of love and compassion. In another experience he underwent what he described as a temporary soul-swap or consciousness expansion, briefly inhabiting the awareness of a being called Orion, experiencing existence at a frequency far beyond normal human perception.

What distinguished Angelucci from contemporaries like Adamski was the explicitly mystical and Christ-like framing of his contacts. He described receiving teachings about humanity's cosmic purpose, the reality of the soul's immortality, and a warning that Earth stood at a decisive juncture. His beings spoke of a cosmic tragedy — a "fallen" element in humanity's history — and of humanity's potential redemption through spiritual awakening rather than merely technological advancement.

The Mystical Dimension

Unlike most contactees, Angelucci dwelt extensively on the psychological and spiritual transformation his experiences catalysed. He described periods of profound elation followed by deep despair — what contemporary researchers would recognise as the classic destabilisation arc of high-strangeness contact. He spoke of feeling fundamentally alienated from ordinary human life after his encounters, convinced he had glimpsed a reality so far beyond the mundane that normal existence felt imprisoning.

Researcher Jacques Vallée treated Angelucci's case as among the most psychologically and phenomenologically interesting in the contactee literature, noting that unlike other contactees, Angelucci showed signs of genuine distress and disorientation rather than the confident showmanship of figures like Adamski. Vallée argued that Angelucci's reports contained structural elements — the voice in the car, the consciousness alteration, the message of spiritual urgency — that recur across global contact traditions in ways that suggest a real underlying phenomenon rather than deliberate fabrication.

Legacy and Influence

Orfeo Angelucci occupies a unique position in UFO history as the bridge between the contactee tradition and the New Age spiritual movement. His emphasis on consciousness transformation, love as the central cosmic force, and humanity's participation in a vast spiritual drama prefigured themes that would dominate channelling movements from the 1970s onward. Writers including Whitley Strieber have acknowledged Angelucci's influence in articulating the inner, transformative quality of genuine contact experiences.

His case also anticipates what researchers like John Mack and Kenneth Ring would later identify as the "omega point" effect of abduction and contact — the way such experiences, regardless of their ultimate origin, consistently push witnesses toward expanded cosmological awareness and diminished fear of death. Angelucci died in 1993 having spent four decades quietly insisting on the reality and spiritual significance of what happened to him in the summer of 1952.

"I know that I am not the same person I was before those nights. Something entered me that I cannot name, and I am not sure I would give it back if I could."
— Orfeo Angelucci, The Secret of the Saucers
