The Khasarov Mirror

A reflective chamber from Russian consciousness research — rooted in Nikolai Kozyrev's torsion field theory and explored by Soviet and post-Soviet scientists as a tool for altering perception, time experience, and non-local awareness.

✦ Important Disclaimer

⚠️

For Educational & Informational Purposes Only

The information on this page is provided solely for educational and informational purposes. It is not medical, psychological, or therapeutic advice. The Khasarov/Kozyrev mirror is an experimental device from Russian parapsychology research that has not been validated by peer-reviewed mainstream science. Extended use of enclosed reflective chambers can produce sensory deprivation effects, altered states of consciousness, and in rare cases disorientation, anxiety, or panic. Please read the following carefully before proceeding.

⛔ Do NOT Use If You Have:

  • Claustrophobia (fear of enclosed spaces)
  • PTSD or a history of significant trauma
  • Psychosis, schizophrenia, or a family history thereof
  • Dissociative disorders (DID, depersonalisation, derealisation)
  • Significant anxiety disorder or panic disorder
  • Cardiovascular or respiratory conditions

🔒 Essential Safety Rules

  • NEVER use alone. Always have another person present outside the chamber who can assist you.
  • Establish a pre-agreed signal (e.g., three knocks) to indicate you want to exit immediately.
  • Keep initial sessions to 15–20 minutes maximum.
  • Medical supervision is strongly recommended for extended use.
  • Do not use while taking medication that affects perception, consciousness, or cardiovascular function.

The research described on this page comes primarily from Russian-language sources and has not been replicated in Western peer-reviewed contexts. Treat all reported effects with appropriate critical perspective.

✦ What Is the Khasarov Mirror?

The device known in Russian consciousness research as the Khasarov mirror is a variant of the broader category of Kozyrev mirrors — a family of reflective structures developed in Soviet and post-Soviet scientific research into consciousness, torsion fields, and non-local information transfer. The two names are often used interchangeably in Western literature, though they refer to slightly different designs and research lineages.

The name Kozyrev mirror derives from Nikolai Aleksandrovich Kozyrev (1908–1983), a Soviet astrophysicist who proposed that time is not merely a dimension of measurement but a physical substance — a carrier of energy and information that flows through the universe and can be focused, reflected, and concentrated, much like light. Kozyrev hypothesised the existence of a form of energy he called torsion radiation — a spiral, rotating field carried by the medium of time itself — which he believed could be detected, reflected, and concentrated using specially shaped metallic structures.

Based on Kozyrev's theoretical framework, later researchers — most notably Alexander Trofimov and Vlail Kaznacheev at the Institute of Clinical and Experimental Medicine in Novosibirsk — developed the cylindrical and spiral reflective chambers that became known as Kozyrev mirrors. These structures are typically made of aluminium sheets rolled into a cylinder or spiral approximately 0.7–1 metre in diameter and 1.4–2 metres tall — large enough for a person to sit inside.

The theoretical purpose of the mirror structure is to focus and amplify torsion radiation — creating an altered electromagnetic and informational environment inside the chamber. Proponents claim that this environment can affect human consciousness in measurable ways: altering EEG brainwave patterns, enhancing extrasensory perception, modifying the subjective experience of time, and facilitating what researchers describe as non-local consciousness phenomena.

It is worth noting that torsion field theory is not accepted by mainstream physics. Kozyrev's original papers generated significant controversy in the Soviet scientific community, and torsion radiation as he described it has not been confirmed by independent experiment. The effects reported in Trofimov and Kaznacheev's studies — while intriguing — come from non-blinded, non-peer-reviewed research, many publications existing only in Russian.

✦ History & The People Behind It

Nikolai Kozyrev (1908–1983)

Nikolai Aleksandrovich Kozyrev was one of the Soviet Union's most unconventional and brilliant astrophysicists. Born in Saint Petersburg, he survived the Stalinist purges, spent years in the Gulag, and emerged to make genuine contributions to astrophysics — most notably his 1958 observation of volcanic activity on the Moon (confirming the Moon is not geologically dead) and his work on stellar energy generation.

Kozyrev's most controversial work concerned the physical properties of time. He proposed that time is not a passive background dimension but an active, causal force — a physical substance with a "density" that can vary, and that flows through the universe in a directional, asymmetric manner. He believed this property of time could be detected by sensitive instruments and that certain physical processes (including human consciousness) were sensitive to the local "density" or "flow rate" of time.

Kozyrev designed experiments using gyroscopes, sensitive torsion balances, and vibrating strings to detect and measure what he called torsion radiation — the medium through which time's physical properties propagated. His results, published in Soviet academic journals during the 1960s–1970s, were contested but attracted serious attention from a minority of Russian physicists and eventually from researchers interested in consciousness.

Trofimov & Kaznacheev — The Novosibirsk Research

The development of Kozyrev mirrors as tools for consciousness research is primarily associated with Alexander V. Trofimov and Vlail P. Kaznacheev, working at the Institute of Clinical and Experimental Medicine in Novosibirsk, Siberia, from the 1980s through the 2000s.

Kaznacheev, a prominent Soviet biomedical researcher known for his earlier work on cellular communication, became interested in non-local biological and informational effects in the 1970s. Together with Trofimov, he developed the hypothesis that aluminium reflective chambers — by focusing Kozyrev's proposed torsion fields — could create conditions favourable to non-local information transfer and altered states of consciousness.

Their reported experiments, conducted at the Arctic Biomedical Research Centre and later at various Siberian facilities, claimed remarkable results: subjects seated inside Kozyrev mirrors showed altered EEG patterns (increased inter-hemispheric coherence), reported vivid non-ordinary experiences (visions of distant locations or historical events), and in some cases appeared to receive verifiable information about distant targets. These were described as part of a broader programme of Russian government-funded parapsychological research.

It should be noted that these results have not been independently replicated in controlled Western research, and the primary literature remains largely in Russian without full English translations. They are presented here as a historical and cultural phenomenon, not as established science.

✦ The Theory

Kozyrev's Torsion Field Hypothesis

Kozyrev's central hypothesis was that time is a physical substance — not merely a dimension of measurement — possessing properties such as density, viscosity, and a directional flow. He argued that the asymmetry of time (its apparent one-directionality — past to future, not future to past) was a physical property caused by this substance's structure, and that physical processes that produce or absorb energy also interact with the local density of time.

The corollary was that torsion radiation — the rotating, spiralling energetic disturbance created when time's flow interacts with rotating physical objects (including astronomical bodies, turbines, gyroscopes, and even DNA, which has a helical structure) — should be detectable and manipulable. Kozyrev believed that aluminium was particularly effective at reflecting and concentrating torsion radiation because of its specific electromagnetic and crystalline properties.

Trofimov and Kaznacheev extended this to argue that human consciousness itself generates and is sensitive to torsion fields. The brain, they hypothesised, is not merely a receiver and processor of electrochemical information but a torsion field generator — and the human nervous system is therefore potentially sensitive to torsion radiation from distant sources. This theoretical leap provided the framework for the Kozyrev mirror as a tool for enhancing non-local perception.

Reported Mechanisms & Effects

Russian researchers reported that subjects seated inside Kozyrev mirrors showed distinctive changes in EEG measurements. Specifically, they claimed increased inter-hemispheric coherence — a synchronisation of alpha and theta wave activity between the left and right brain hemispheres similar to that observed in experienced meditators and some altered states. This was interpreted as evidence that the mirror environment was affecting the brain's electromagnetic field.

Subjects also reported altered time perception — a subjective sense that time was passing unusually slowly or quickly, or that temporal boundaries between past, present, and future became less defined. Some subjects reported experiences interpreted as access to historical events or future impressions.

A significant confounding factor in all this research — acknowledged by critical reviewers — is that enclosed reflective spaces naturally produce sensory deprivation effects regardless of any torsion field involvement. Reduced sensory input, visual distortion from reflective surfaces, and mild hypoxia in poorly ventilated chambers can all produce altered states, unusual perceptions, and vivid imagery through entirely conventional neurological mechanisms.

🔬 Scientific Status

Torsion field theory as proposed by Kozyrev is not accepted by mainstream physics. It is classified as a fringe science claim. Some Russian and post-Soviet researchers continue to publish work on torsion fields, but this research has not been replicated under Western peer-review conditions. The consciousness effects of Kozyrev mirrors remain scientifically unexplained and unconfirmed.

✦ How Researchers Used It

Research Session Protocol

In the Novosibirsk research framework, sessions with Kozyrev mirrors were conducted under clinical conditions with medical monitoring. The protocol described in available Russian sources typically proceeded as follows:

  1. 1.

    The subject was seated inside or positioned near the reflective aluminium cylinder. Some experimental designs used a half-cylinder behind the subject (open to the front) rather than a fully enclosing structure.

  2. 2.

    EEG electrodes were attached to monitor brainwave activity in real time. Some sessions included heart rate, skin conductance, and other physiological monitoring.

  3. 3.

    Sessions typically lasted 20–90 minutes. Subjects were instructed to relax, close their eyes, and allow impressions to arise without directing them. Some protocols involved specific meditative or attentional instructions.

  4. 4.

    Post-session, subjects were interviewed about their experiences: any visions, emotional states, time distortion, unusual perceptions, or apparent non-local impressions. These were correlated with the EEG data.

Reported Experimental Results

Among the results reported (but not independently replicated) from Russian mirror research:

Apparent Remote Perception

Some subjects reported vivid, detailed impressions of distant locations that researchers claimed correlated with actual places or events. These reports were not conducted under the double-blind conditions required for scientific validity.

Apparent Contact with Deceased Individuals

Several subjects reported experiences interpreted as contact with deceased people, including the reception of seemingly verifiable information. These reports are anecdotal and cannot be assessed for accuracy without controlled methodology.

Historical & Future Impressions

Consistent with Kozyrev's theory that the mirror affects time perception, some subjects reported impressions interpreted as historical events or future scenarios. These are among the most difficult claims to evaluate scientifically.

Measurable EEG Changes

The most potentially replicable finding — reported changes in inter-hemispheric EEG coherence — is also the most easily attributed to sensory deprivation and relaxation response, independent of any torsion field effect.

✦ How Enthusiasts Attempt It — A Simplified Version

Reminder: The following describes how enthusiasts have attempted DIY versions of the Kozyrev mirror at home. This is provided for informational completeness only. Always prioritise safety. Never attempt alone. Keep sessions short, especially as a beginner.

🛠️ Basic DIY Construction

The most common DIY approach involves creating a simple cylindrical or spiral reflective chamber from aluminium foil wrapped around a lightweight frame. The dimensions described in Russian research suggest a diameter of approximately 70 cm (28 inches) and a height of approximately 140 cm (55 inches) — sufficient to seat a person cross-legged or on a low stool inside.

📐

Frame

A cylinder of PVC pipe or cardboard tubing, or a frame of lightweight bamboo canes, forms the structure. The cylinder approach (as opposed to spiral) is simpler to construct.

🪙

Reflective Surface

Heavy-duty aluminium foil (or thin aluminium sheet) is attached to the frame with the reflective side facing inward. Ensure adequate ventilation — a fully sealed chamber is dangerous. Leave the top open or use a mesh top.

🪑

Seating

A low stool, cushion, or meditation pillow placed inside the cylinder for comfort. Ensure you can stand and exit quickly without difficulty.

📋 Session Guidelines for Beginners

  1. 1.

    Ensure safety. Your companion must be seated immediately outside the chamber, able to see or hear you at all times. Agree on an exit signal (three knocks on the frame) before you begin.

  2. 2.

    Set a timer. For beginners: 15–20 minutes maximum. Your companion should check in verbally every 5 minutes. Do not exceed 30 minutes for any session without prior experience.

  3. 3.

    Enter with relaxed, open intention. Sit comfortably, close your eyes, and breathe slowly. Allow impressions to arise without forcing them. No specific goal or outcome is required.

  4. 4.

    Exit calmly. When the timer sounds or you are ready to exit, take a moment before standing. Ground yourself with slow, deliberate breaths before stepping out.

  5. 5.

    Journal immediately. Write down everything you experienced — physical sensations, emotional states, visual impressions, time perception, thoughts — while the memory is fresh. This is valuable data regardless of its interpretation.

✦ What To Expect

The most commonly reported effects of sitting inside a reflective chamber are the predictable consequences of mild sensory deprivation. The visual field is dominated by the reflective surfaces, which create unusual patterns of light and shadow. Ambient sound is reduced and slightly distorted by the aluminium walls. The physical enclosure creates a sense of separation from ordinary environmental stimuli.

Under these conditions — which are present regardless of any torsion field effect — the brain's pattern-recognition systems naturally become more active and "inward-directed." Phosphenes (visual patterns generated by the visual cortex in the absence of external light input) become more pronounced. Mild hypnagogic imagery may arise. Introspective states deepen. The subjective sense of the passage of time frequently changes.

Practitioners who go beyond these baseline sensory deprivation effects and experience something more striking — vivid imagery, apparent non-local impressions, or unusual temporal experiences — may be experiencing enhanced access to the hypnagogic state, placebo and expectation effects, or (if one accepts the theoretical framework) genuine torsion field effects. These possibilities cannot be distinguished without controlled experimental conditions.

👁️ Visual Effects

Phosphenes, geometric patterns, shifting colours, or more complex imagery as visual cortex activity increases in reduced external input. Some practitioners report vivid scenes, faces, or landscapes.

⏱️ Time Distortion

Both time dilation (sessions feeling much shorter than they are) and time compression (sessions feeling much longer) are reported. Some subjects describe a complete loss of temporal orientation.

🔮 Heightened Introspection

Deep meditative states with unusually vivid internal experience. Enhanced access to memories, emotional material, or creative insight.

🌡️ Physical Sensations

Mild tingling, warmth, or unusual pressure sensations reported by some practitioners. These are consistent with sensory deprivation and heightened somatic awareness.

💭 Apparent Non-Local Impressions

A minority of practitioners report vivid impressions interpreted as perception of distant events, deceased individuals, or non-ordinary states of awareness. These should be treated with critical reflection rather than literal belief.

✦ What To Avoid

🚫

Using the Mirror Alone

This is the most important rule. If you experience any difficulty — panic, disorientation, nausea, chest tightness — while alone inside the chamber, there is no one to assist you. Never enter without a companion physically present outside.

🚫

Exceeding Recommended Time Without Check-Ins

Sensory deprivation effects become more pronounced and harder to manage after 30 minutes. Do not attempt extended sessions until you have multiple short sessions under your belt. Always maintain regular verbal check-ins with your companion.

🚫

Using When Claustrophobic

Even mild claustrophobia can become acute inside an enclosed reflective chamber. Do not minimise this risk. If you have any history of discomfort in enclosed spaces, the Kozyrev mirror is not an appropriate practice.

🚫

Using Medication That Affects Perception

Psychoactive medications, benzodiazepines, antidepressants, sleep aids, and many other common medications can alter the sensory deprivation experience in unpredictable ways. Always consult your prescribing physician before attempting any practice that induces altered states.

🚫

Interpreting Visions as Literal Reality

Whatever you experience inside the chamber — however vivid or compelling — requires critical reflection before being treated as factual information about the world. Vivid imagery and apparent knowledge can be generated by the brain in altered states without any paranormal mechanism. Maintain the observer's distance.

🚫

Poorly Ventilated Chambers

A fully sealed enclosure with a person inside will accumulate CO₂ rapidly. Ensure your chamber has an open top or adequate ventilation at all times. Mild hypoxia can produce altered states that mimic genuinely interesting effects — but it is simply oxygen deprivation and is potentially dangerous.

✦ When To Stop — Exit Immediately If:

🛑 Panic or Chest Tightness

Any experience of panic, chest tightness, difficulty breathing, or heart palpitations is a clear signal to exit immediately. Use your pre-agreed signal and exit calmly but promptly.

🛑 Nausea or Disorientation

Nausea, dizziness, or significant disorientation (inability to re-orient yourself after exiting) should be taken seriously. Rest, drink water, and seek medical assessment if symptoms persist.

🛑 Persistent Paranoia or Intrusive Thoughts

If sessions consistently produce paranoid thinking, intrusive imagery, or thoughts that persist after you exit the chamber, stop the practice entirely and speak with a mental health professional.

🌐 Secondary Sources & Context

Russian & Soviet Parapsychology — Historical Context

For the broader context of Soviet-era research into consciousness, psychical phenomena, and parapsychology, Loren Graham's work on Soviet science and Oleg Sarin's writings on Russian consciousness research provide accessible English-language overviews. The Soviet government funded significant parapsychology research, partly as a strategic response to perceived US interest in psychical intelligence applications.

Sensory Deprivation Research — Western Counterpart

For the scientifically validated effects of enclosed, low-stimulus environments on consciousness, Western research on floatation REST (Restricted Environmental Stimulation Technique) — notably John C. Lilly's foundational work and more recent clinical research — provides a rigorous, peer-reviewed framework for understanding the baseline effects of sensory deprivation, independent of any torsion field hypothesis.

📋 A Note on Sources

The majority of primary literature on Kozyrev mirrors and torsion field consciousness research exists in Russian and has not been peer-reviewed in Western academic contexts. Much of what circulates in English is secondary summaries of varying reliability. Approach all claims about torsion fields, consciousness effects, and non-local perception with the same critical standards you would apply to any extraordinary claim — extraordinary evidence is required for extraordinary conclusions.
