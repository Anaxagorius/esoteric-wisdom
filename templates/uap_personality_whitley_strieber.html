Whitley Strieber

"The Communion Witness" · b. 1945

Brief Biography

Louis Whitley Strieber was born on 13 June 1945 in San Antonio, Texas. He studied at the University of Texas at Austin and the London Film School, and built a career as a novelist — primarily in the horror genre, with notable works including The Wolfen (1978) and The Hunger (1981), both of which were adapted into films. He was a successful literary figure with no prior connection to the UFO field when his life was apparently transformed by events beginning on the night of 26 December 1985, at his cabin in upstate New York.

His 1987 memoir Communion: A True Story, which described his encounter experiences with non-human entities he called "the visitors," became one of the best-selling non-fiction books in American publishing history, spending 26 weeks on the New York Times bestseller list and selling millions of copies worldwide. The cover image — artist Ted Seth Jacobs's depiction of a large-eyed non-human face — became one of the most recognisable images in modern popular culture and defined the visual archetype of the grey alien for a generation.

The Communion Experience

Strieber's account described being removed from his cabin in the night by small, large-eyed beings — the "visitors" — and subjected to a series of intrusive physical and psychological procedures. He underwent hypnotic regression with Budd Hopkins's collaborators and later with independent therapists, and documented his experiences in a series of books: Transformation (1988), Breakthrough (1995), The Secret School (1997), and others. Uniquely among abduction experiencers, Strieber brought both a novelist's literary sophistication and a deep sceptical uncertainty to his account: he consistently declined to assert a confident interpretation of what had happened to him, instead presenting the experience as something vast, terrifying, and genuinely beyond his comprehension.

His visitors did not fit neatly into the conventional abduction narrative framework — they were stranger, more philosophically challenging, and more ambiguous than the medical-procedure-focused greys of Hopkins or Jacobs. Strieber increasingly interpreted them not as extraterrestrials but as some form of intelligence intimately connected with human consciousness, death, and evolutionary transformation — an interpretation closer to John Keel or Jacques Vallée than to the nuts-and-bolts ET hypothesis.

Unknown Country and Cultural Legacy

Strieber founded Unknown Country, a website and podcast platform for discussing anomalous phenomena, which continues to operate. He lost his wife and co-host Anne Strieber in 2015, an event he described publicly as devastating and which deepened his exploration of consciousness and what lies beyond death. His contributions to the UAP field are primarily cultural — he shifted the encounter narrative into the mainstream literary imagination and created a framework for discussing non-human contact that millions of people have used to process their own anomalous experiences.
